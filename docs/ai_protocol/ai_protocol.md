# ERM 424 AI Protocol

This page is the course's shared policy for AI-assisted work in graded assignments. It is first used in Module 3, Clean It Twice Part B, and every later two-part assignment reuses it. AI means artificial intelligence; an assistant is any AI tool that proposes text, code or analysis.

## 1. This protocol and your AI Working Agreement

In week 1 you drafted a personal AI Working Agreement, your own rules for AI use. This protocol is different: it is the course's shared policy for graded work, the same for everyone. The two operate together. The protocol sets the floor for assessed assignments, including the requirement that Part B tasks use an AI assistant under your supervision, because auditing AI-produced work is itself a skill this course teaches and grades. Your agreement continues to govern everything discretionary, and it may be stricter than this protocol anywhere outside those requirements. You will revise your agreement near the end of the course, with your accumulated disclosure forms as the record of what you actually did.

## 2. The course policy

### The working stance: the assistant proposes, you verify, you sign

In every Part B task the assistant works as a junior analyst and you work as the senior analyst who reviews and signs. The assistant proposes. You verify every operation against the data and against what you know about it. You decide what is accepted, modified or rejected, and you sign the disclosure. What you submit is your work, including the parts the assistant drafted, because you decided to keep them. The assistant's output is never graded; your audit of it is.

### What allowable use looks like

Where the assignment asks for it (every Part B), use the assistant for the task as instructed, starting from the standard role prompt. Elsewhere in graded work, the test is whether you would accept the same help from a colleague who has not seen the data: a syntax reminder, an explanation of an error message, the name of a pandas method, a rephrasing of a sentence you wrote. Three conditions apply to anything you keep: you can explain every line of it, you have checked it against the data, and you disclose it.

Part A of a two-part assignment is the manual route, and the comparison between the two routes is what the assignment teaches; producing Part A with an assistant defeats it and is not allowed.

### What must always be disclosed

Every AI contribution to graded work: which tool, how you reached it, what you asked, what you gave it, and what you kept. In Part B the disclosure form is the mechanism. Outside Part B, one line at the point of use is enough: a code comment or a sentence naming the tool and what it contributed. Discussion posts and replies are graded work, so the same rule applies to them. If you are unsure whether something needs disclosing, disclose it: an unneeded disclosure costs nothing, and a missing one is a violation of this protocol.

### What is out of bounds

- Submitting assistant output you have not run and checked yourself.
- Presenting assistant-produced work as your own without disclosure.
- Giving an assistant the course's private material (answer keys, other students' work) or anything confidential from your employer.
- Using an assistant to do the manual route of a two-part assignment (Part A).

Northwestern's academic integrity policy applies to all of this; the protocol says how it applies here.

## 4. The role prompt

Each two-part assignment ships with a standard role prompt, a short fixed text you give the assistant before anything else, so that differences between students' Part B results reflect auditing judgment rather than prompt wording. For Module 3 it is [the Module 3 role prompt](role_prompt_m3.md). Use it exactly as provided; anything you want to add goes in your follow-up prompts and is disclosed. The role prompt does not include the data dictionary, on purpose: what you know about the data is your advantage as the auditor, and the file says so.

## 5. The disclosure form

Every Part B deliverable includes [the disclosure form](disclosure_template.md): six fixed fields, about five minutes to complete. The Clean It Twice notebook carries the same six fields in its Part B cells, so for Module 3 you complete them there. The form is how the course meets Northwestern's expectations on AI disclosure, and your accumulated forms are the record you will revise your Working Agreement against in week 9.

Part B grades the audit, not the output: points attach primarily to the critique memo and secondarily to the disposition-of-output table; the other fields are graded for completion.
