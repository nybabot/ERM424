# The Desk Board: Analyst Note template and week 5 topics

From week 5 through week 9 the course discussion is your desk's working board. Each Thursday you post two things: your three-line position and your Analyst Note. The notes thread is gated: you see nothing on it until you post.

## The Thursday position (three lines)

```
Frequency call:              (Poisson or negative binomial, and the one number that decides it)
Leading severity candidate:  (the distribution, and its main caveat)
Top fitness gap:             (the gap, and what it does to your analysis)
```

## The Analyst Note (half a page)

```
Topic:        (one topic from this week's menu; not one a desk-mate has taken)
Author:       (your name)
Extension:    (one line: what this note does beyond what the assignment requires)

Exhibit:      (one chart or table to the Module 4 standard: a title that states the finding,
               labeled axes with units, a scale that does not mislead, readable without color,
               and a two-sentence caption: what it shows; what it hides or what a reader
               should not conclude)

Conclusion:   (one stated conclusion the exhibit supports, in a sentence or two)
```

Reusing your assignment pipeline and code is expected and right. Credit attaches to the extension, the exhibit and the conclusion, not to the recycled computation.

**Coverage.** Your desk coordinates topics in the open desk chat, Monday to Wednesday: all four topics covered, no repeats. A three-person desk covers three.

## Week 5 note topics

1. Concentration: your line's twenty largest events, their share of total loss, the mean with and without them, and what breaks for anyone planning on the average.
2. Arrival pattern: are losses random or clustered: annual counts, variance-to-mean, the Poisson-versus-negative-binomial evidence laid out.
3. The cost of the flaw: size your line's most consequential data quirk in numbers, results with it unhandled versus handled.
4. Fragility: refit severity under a defensible alternative preparation choice; report how far the parameters move and whether your conclusion survives.

## The rest of the week

- **Friday:** this week's desk lead posts the Desk Summary: 3 to 5 sentences synthesizing the desk's notes, including the required field, where the notes disagree or sit in tension.
- **Friday to Sunday:** your cross-desk critique on one other desk's board, under three rules: it contains a question that desk has not answered; it anchors to something specific in a note or summary; and it raises a point earlier critiques have not. Within your own desk, replies are welcome but must challenge the ranking or the tension named in the Desk Summary.

Nothing your desk-mates do or fail to do affects your grade: a missing note appears as a missing name.
