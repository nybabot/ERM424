# Lakefill National Bank

**Office of the Chief Risk Officer** · Chicago, Illinois

April 6, 2026

To: the engagement team

**Subject: Operational loss exposure engagement, sponsor letter**

Thank you for taking this on. The Board Risk Committee has asked my office for an independent view of the bank's operational loss exposure before the next capital planning cycle, and I want that view to come from people who will look at the data first and tell us what it does and does not support. This letter sets out what the committee asked, what you will receive, and what I expect.

**The committee's three questions**

1. What does our operational loss experience look like, for the bank and for each business line, and can the data behind it be trusted?
2. What drives our largest and most frequent losses?
3. How should we monitor this exposure going forward?

None of these has a one-week answer. I would rather have an honest partial answer with its limits stated than a complete-looking one that does not survive the committee's questions.

**Two levels of coverage**

Every analyst on the team covers the whole bank and one assigned business line in depth. The bank-wide view is shared work; the line is yours. I expect each of you to say where your line's experience differs from the bank's and what that difference changes, for the line's management and for the committee. If your line looks like the bank, say so and show why; that is a finding too. My office will confirm the desks, their lines and their members in a short coverage memo once line assignments are settled.

**What you will receive**

The operational loss event register for 2021 to 2025, extracted from our operational risk management system (ORMS) as of 2026-03-31, with a monthly exposure table for each business line and a data dictionary. The register is issued once, as it came out of the system; it has not been cleaned for you, and it will not change. The dictionary records how the data was collected, including the date ORMS went live (2023-01-01) and the other changes since then that affect what the register can show. Read it before you trust any number.

**Timeline**

The engagement runs in six stages; the course week for each is in parentheses.

| Stage | What I expect to see |
|---|---|
| 1 (week 5) | Your engagement charter, a judgment of whether the data is fit for the purpose your charter states, and first candidate models for how often losses arrive and how large they run |
| 2 (week 6) | A first claim about your line, registered before you test it, and tested with stated assumptions; a finding of no effect, defended, is a full answer |
| 3 (week 7) | The drivers of loss size and loss frequency, bank-wide and for your line |
| 4 (week 8) | An early-warning model for losses that will become major, with a threshold priced by what a miss and a false alarm each cost, and your sign-off judgment on it |
| 5 (week 9) | An audit of the classifier the bank already uses, and a monitoring design scored on the first quarter of 2026 |
| 6 (week 10) | A briefing for my office and the committee, checked against a refreshed extract as of 2026-06-30 |

**What I expect from your work**

State your assumptions before your conclusions. Tell me the caveats that would change a decision, not every caveat you can think of. Where the data cannot answer a question, say so and say what would. I sign what goes to the committee, so I will read the reasoning as closely as the numbers.

I look forward to your charter.

Dana Whitfield
Chief Risk Officer (CRO), Lakefill National Bank
